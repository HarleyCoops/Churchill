Valuation Brief

Churchill “The Second World War” — Cassell First Edition, First Printing, Complete Set

Item Description

Complete six-volume first edition, first printing of Winston S. Churchill’s The Second World War (Cassell and Company Ltd., London, 1948–1954). Black cloth boards with gilt spine titles, red top edges, decorative endpapers, all original dust jackets present with correct first-issue pricing (25s. NET for Vols I–IV, 30s. NET for Vols V–VI). All folding maps and diagrams present as called for.

Volume I contains a signed autograph letter from Winston Churchill, dated 6 December 1946 from Chartwell, Westerham, Kent, addressed to Colonel Bryan Charles Fairfax, C.M.G. The letter thanks Fairfax for a “kind letter” and is affixed to the front of the volume.

Provenance Chain

Churchill writes letter at Chartwell (6 Dec 1946) → Col. Fairfax, Toronto → Fairfax estate (d. 29 Jan 1950) → Canadian Federal Government facility, BC → Government auction → Current owner

Vault-stored condition with no aging or creasing of covers is consistent with institutional/government storage. The Fairfax family connection to Toronto (marriage into the Gooderham family; son became 14th Lord Fairfax of Cameron) provides a plausible path from private collection to Canadian government custody as possible unclaimed or escheated property.

Basis for Estimate

The set sits in the upper tier of Churchill first edition values due to the combination of: (1) a genuine autograph letter signed, (2) a historically documented and verifiable recipient, (3) a compelling narrative backed by primary sources, and (4) exceptional vault-stored condition. The letter pre-dates the first volume’s publication by eighteen months, adding historical interest.

The lower bound reflects conservative auction results for Churchill ALS sets without aggressive marketing. The upper bound accounts for the Fairfax military narrative, the French citations, the government provenance, and the potential for the set to attract both Churchill collectors and WWI military history collectors simultaneously.

Upside Factors

If the original Fairfax letter to Churchill is located in the Churchill Archives Centre (Cambridge), it would complete the correspondence and potentially push the value above the stated range. The Fairfax family connection to the 14th Lord Fairfax of Cameron and the Gooderham family of Toronto adds aristocratic and Canadian industrial provenance that specialist buyers may value.

This brief is an informal research estimate, not a professional appraisal. For auction purposes, consult a specialist at Christie’s, Sotheby’s, or Bonhams (Books and Manuscripts departments), or a certified appraiser with expertise in Churchill first editions.

Recommended Next Steps

1. Professional Appraisal. Commission a formal appraisal from a certified specialist in Churchill first editions. Peter Harrington (London), Shapero Rare Books, or the Books and Manuscripts department at a major auction house are appropriate choices.

2. Archive Search. Contact the Churchill Archives Centre at Churchill College, Cambridge, to search for incoming correspondence from Colonel Fairfax (November–December 1946). A discovered letter would materially increase value and historical significance.

3. Conservation Assessment. If selling at auction, have the dust jackets and letter assessed by a paper conservator. The vault-stored condition is a selling point and should be professionally documented.

4. Auction Strategy. Consider a specialist Churchill or military history sale rather than a general books sale. The set’s narrative (Somme, gas poisoning, French citations, Chartwell) is strong marketing material and may attract competitive bidding from both Churchill and WWI collectors.
