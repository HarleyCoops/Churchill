The Chartwell Connection

Thirty years after these events, on 6 December 1946, Winston Churchill wrote from Chartwell in Kent to Colonel Fairfax, thanking him for a “kind letter.” The reply was not a courtesy to a stranger — Churchill and Fairfax shared overlapping spheres across three decades of military service, War Office hierarchy, and Conservative politics.

“He went to an absolute shadow of himself, and although he wanted to stop on with the Battalion, I had to be quite firm and told him that it was out of the question.”

The letter was later affixed inside Volume I of Churchill’s “The Second World War” first edition set — likely by Fairfax himself or a family member as a keepsake. The set was eventually purchased from the Canadian Federal Government at auction.

The Chartwell marker (J) on the map inset connects this battlefield route to the December 1946 letter, bridging the Somme of 1916 to Churchill’s study at Chartwell three decades later.
