From the Somme to Chartwell.

A source-backed chronology connecting Colonel Bryan Charles Fairfax, the 89th Brigade history, the National Library of Scotland trench maps, and Churchill’s signed 1946 reply now affixed to Volume I.

Evidence standardEach event below is tied to the project ledger, artifact photography, the 89th Brigade history, or NLS map source imagery.
BoundaryThe page avoids claims about Churchill and Fairfax’s private relationship beyond the documented 1946 letter exchange and known institutional overlap.

Chronology
1873

Birth of Bryan Charles Fairfax

Colonel Bryan Charles Fairfax, C.M.G., was born 12 September 1873.

Ledger #1Readme.md
1900

Boxer Uprising service

Fairfax’s earlier military career included service in China during the Boxer Uprising, after service in the King’s Own Yorkshire Light Infantry and the West India Regiment.

Ledger #2–4Readme.md
28 Jun 1916

Extreme right of the British Army

The 89th Brigade occupied the junction with the French Corps de Fer. General Congreve visited before the offensive.

“I know you will, and if you cannot manage it, no other troops can.”
Ledger #11, #4389th Bde p.129
1 Jul 1916

Maricourt: Fairfax and Lepetit advance together

At the opening of the Somme, Fairfax commanded the 17th Battalion King’s Liverpool Regiment. In the second wave he advanced arm in arm with Commandant Lepetit of the neighbouring French unit.

Ledger #12, #14Maricourt89th Bde p.131
1 Jul 1916

Montauban and the Briqueterie objective

The brigade history records the attack through Montauban and the taking of the Briqueterie. The associated fixed map crop preserves the objective area as source cartography.

Ledger #13BriqueterieNLS 101465287
11 Jul 1916

French letter names Fairfax

Colonel de Coutard’s official letter singled Fairfax out by name and praised his military qualities and the cordial relations maintained with the French.

“leur chef le Colonel Fairfax … ses hautes qualités militaires”
Ledger #1589th Bde p.134
Mid-Jul 1916

Trones Wood: dug in and holding

Stanley’s account places Fairfax in Trones Wood, with his men dug in after an awful time and able to hold on. The northern NLS source sheet supplies the readable Trones Wood crop.

“Fairfax was in the wood … were all dug down, and could hold on.”
Ledger #16NLS 10146477789th Bde p.143
29–30 Jul 1916

Guillemont: gas, fog, and command endurance

On the way to the Guillemont assembly position Fairfax was affected by a new gas shell, then continued through the attack. Stanley later wrote that Fairfax became “an absolute shadow of himself” and had to be ordered away.

Ledger #17–19TextExtract.md lines 29, 49
1916

C.M.G. awarded

Fairfax was awarded Companion of the Order of St Michael and St George for wartime service.

Ledger #7Readme.md
6 Dec 1946

Churchill replies from Chartwell

The signed note from Winston Churchill, dated 6 December 1946, thanks Fairfax for a “kind letter.” It is now affixed to Volume I of the Cassell first-edition set.

Ledger #26–27Images/signature.JPGChartwell
1948

The Gathering Storm appears

Volume I of Churchill’s The Second World War was first published by Cassell in 1948. The copyright page and first-issue jacket details are documented photographically.

Ledger #28–32FirstPublished.JPGPrice.JPG
1950

Fairfax dies in Toronto

Fairfax died in Toronto on 29 January 1950. Later volumes in the complete set would therefore have been assembled after his death.
