04 · Why Churchill Knew HimFive documented threads
Why Churchill Knew Him

Not a courtesy to a stranger.

Overlapping spheres across three decades — front line, ministry, labour corps, party, and the Commonwealth elite.

01
Western Front, 1915–16
Churchill commanded the 6th Royal Scots Fusiliers at Ploegsteert Jan–May 1916 — home weeks before the Somme opened.
02
War Office, 1919–21
Secretary of State for War. Fairfax held the post of Director of Remounts in the same building, in the same hierarchy.
03
Chinese Labour Corps
Fairfax commanded the CLC in France (≈100,000 men). Churchill, as Minister of Munitions then War Secretary, oversaw its policy.
04
Conservative Circles
Spen Valley by-election, 1919. Two officers, two Conservatives — one party-political world.
05
Canadian Elite
Married into the Gooderham family of Toronto. His son became the 14th Lord Fairfax of Cameron.

Phase 1 · May – 28 June 191689th Bde · pp. 119, 121, 129
Phase 1

Brigade HQ, in a pit
southwest of Maricourt.

By May the Brigade had moved into the sector. HQ was established at Ailly-sur-Somme, the 17th Battalion at Vaux. Through June, the Brigade advanced to a pit on the hinge of the British and French armies — southwest of Maricourt.

Ten days later

A French Colonel singled Fairfax out by name.

…le courage et le sang-froid de nos voisins, officiers et hommes du 17 bataillon de King's Liverpool. En particulier, les relations si cordiales entretenues avec leur chef le Colonel Fairfax ont permis de remarquer ses hautes qualités militaires…
89th Brigade, p.134 · 11 July 1916 · Ledger #15
Translation

"…the courage and composure of our neighbours, officers and men of the 17th Battalion King's Liverpool. In particular, the cordial relations maintained with their commander Colonel Fairfax revealed his high military qualities — and made all the more regrettable the departure of this brilliant leader and his magnificent Battalion."

Phase 5 · Early August 1916Stanley, personal passage
Phase 5 · "An Absolute Shadow"
…he stuck it out most gallantly at the time of the attack, it was too much for him afterwards and he suffered very much. He went to an absolute shadow of himself, and although he wanted to stop on with the Battalion, I had to be quite firm and told him that it was out of the question. If he had not gone then I don't think that his life would be worth anything at all.

10 · The ReplyChartwell · 6 December 1946
The Reply

From the library at Chartwell
— thirty years on.

Churchill replied from the house where he was then drafting The Gathering Storm. The letter travelled from Chartwell to Toronto, where Fairfax had retired. Fairfax died thirteen months later; the last volumes of the set were assembled after his death.
